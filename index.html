Everything a robustness study needs

From a single call to a full benchmark report — without wiring the parts together yourself.

◉

One entry point

A single wl.evaluate() call runs embedding, visual-quality measurement, the attack sweep, extraction and scoring. It reads the dataloader to decide whether your model is post-generation (images) or in-generation (prompts) and routes the run accordingly.

⚙

44 attack configurations, 7 groups

Compression (classic JPEG / JPEG2000 / WebP and learned BMSHJ2018 / MBT2018 / Cheng2020), adversarial embedding, noise, blur, geometric, colour and diffusion regeneration — each with a calibrated strength sweep, all built by AttackersWithFactorsModel().

▤

34 differentiable attackers

Drop-in DiffAttacker layers for end-to-end adversarial training, including differentiable JPEG (mask / polynomial / Fourier), screen-capture PIMoG and print-capture StegaStamp.

☼

11 watermark models

Seven post-generation (DctDwt, DctDwtSvd, RivaGAN, StegaStamp, TrustMark, InvisMark, VINE), three in-generation (Tree-Ring, GaussianShading, StableSignature) and iSteganoGAN.

▣

10 metrics, 5 dataset loaders

Robustness (BER, EA, NC, NEB, TPR@x%FPR) and imperceptibility (PSNR, SSIM, RMSE, MAE, LPIPS) metrics, plus FID for in-generation runs, over MS-COCO 2017 images and prompts, Kodak24 and USC-SIPI.

▦

21 plotting helpers, 7 base classes

Robustness curves, model and attacker rankings, visual-quality and stego comparisons. Extend BaseWatermarkModel, BaseTestAttackModel, BaseMetric and the other base classes to plug in your own method.

What the benchmark finds

Ranked by cumulative RQ-AUC across the attack sweep. The strongest post-generation method is StegaStamp; in-generation methods lead overall.

Sensitive to cropping — a 10% crop drops TPR@0.1%FPR to 0.01.

  • In-generation methods win overall: mapping the watermark into the latent distribution makes it more evenly spread through the image than a post-hoc perturbation can achieve.
  • Geometric and regeneration attacks are the strongest categories — most current methods remain insufficiently robust against them.
  • GaussianShading and VINE are notably weak under geometric attacks; TrustMark ranks behind StegaStamp under rotation, flipping and regeneration.
  • Among post-generation methods, TrustMark and InvisMark achieve the highest visual quality, while RivaGAN and DctDwtSvd introduce visible colour shifts.

Core modules

Eight modules — jump straight into any part of the API reference.

→watermarks

Ten reference methods for comparative studies — seven post-generation and three in-generation.

→attackers

TestAttacker for benchmarking robustness, and differentiable DiffAttacker for adversarial training and the development of new methods. The default AttackersWithFactorsModel() ships 44 attack configurations; the standard benchmark evaluates 34 of them.

→metrics

Robustness metrics (BER, EA, TPR@x%FPR) and imperceptibility metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FID), plus a base class for custom metrics.

→tools

Auxiliary utilities such as compression coding and reversible data hiding, supporting the development of robust reversible watermarking.

→datasets

Mainstream benchmarks — MS-COCO 2017 images and captions, Kodak24 and USC-SIPI — with the batched loader that pairs data with watermark bits.

→laboratories

The evaluation platform for PGW and IGW methods, together with the base interfaces you implement to plug in your own model, attacker or metric.

→draw

Visualization and interactive analysis: robustness curves, model and attacker rankings, visual-quality plots and stego / attack comparisons.

→steganography

iSteganoGAN, an improved SteganoGAN model that uses L-BFGS for more accurate and stable watermark extraction. It ships alongside the seven core modules and subclasses BaseWatermarkModel.
